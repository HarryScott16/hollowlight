# Readability principles for Hollowlight wards

Research notes on game-art and UI readability, applied to Hollowlight: procedurally drawn vector wards, 36-60 px on screen, a dark teal-green map, a top-down camera, 20+ ward types, several upgrade tiers.

**How sources are marked.** Every claim has a source link. "(verified)" means I read the primary text myself: either I extracted text from the PDF or I fetched the page. "(via summary)" means I only saw a search-engine or secondary summary of the page. "(unverified)" means I could not confirm the claim. Primary sources I read in full or in part:
- Valve's TF2 SIGGRAPH 2007 course notes
- Valve's GDC 2008 TF2 slides
- Valve's Dota 2 Character Art Guide (archived copy)
- Riot's "Clarity in League"
- Healey 1996
- Healey & Enns 2012
- Microsoft's icon guidelines

## 1. Silhouette tests

### What a silhouette test is
You fill each design in as a flat black shape with no interior detail. If it is still identifiable, the shape alone carries its identity. If two designs look alike as blobs, colour or detail has to do work that shape should be doing.

### How studios use it
- **Valve, Team Fortress 2.** The nine classes "were carefully designed to be very distinct from one another". Body proportions, weapons, footwear, hats and clothing folds were "explicitly designed to give each character a unique silhouette." Valve validated the designs with a silhouette-only test during concept: "demonstrating the ability to visually read the characters even with no internal detail was used to validate the character design during the concept phase." Interior details such as clothing folds "echo silhouette shapes in order to emphasize silhouettes". Silhouettes are emphasised "with rim highlights rather than dark outlines". (verified) [SIGGRAPH 2007 course notes, Mitchell/Francke/Eng](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- **Valve's read hierarchy.** The GDC 2008 deck lists three reads in order (verified) [GDC 2008 "Stylization with a Purpose"](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf):
  1. **Team, "Friend or Foe?"**: read through colour.
  2. **Class, "Run or Attack?"**: read through distinctive silhouettes (body proportions, weapons, shoes, hats, clothing folds).
  3. **Selected weapon, "What's he packin'?"**: read through the "highest contrast at chest level, where weapon is held" and a "gradient from dark feet to light chest".

  The same deck orders character creation as "1. Character silhouette, 2. Interior shapes…", calls the silhouette the "building block of character design", and says to "solve design problems using silhouette only".
- **Valve, Dota 2.** Dota 2 uses a high-angle, near-top-down camera, so this guide is close to Hollowlight's case. "A hero's silhouette must be clearly identifiable at first glance. The silhouette should show a character's orientation." "Weapons need a unique read." A section on directionality says "Values, colors, details, and silhouettes help to define each hero's head, their weapon and the direction they are facing." (verified, archived text) [Dota 2 Workshop Character Art Guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935) (originally `support.steampowered.com/kb/9334-YDXV-8590`)
- **Riot, League of Legends.** League is an isometric top-down MOBA. Riot says "Silhouettes are the single most important thing for champion recognition in League." A strong silhouette has "a defining primary characteristic that's unique to that champion", "should highlight their source of power", and makes it "clear which direction a champion is facing". On skins: "A champion's primary feature should never be removed or significantly altered in any skin." (verified) [Riot, "Clarity in League" (2021)](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)
- **Blizzard, Overwatch.** Art director Bill Petras: "Ultimately, the Overwatch art style is focused on readability of gameplay… a handcrafted look for the materials, combined with exaggerated silhouettes for the heroes." (verified) [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/digital-art/blizzards-new-online-game-too-little-too-late-21514147). Arnold Tsang: "You want everything in your visual design to say something about your hero." (verified) [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/without-torbjorn-overwatch-wouldnt-be-overwatch-1792919785). PC Gamer reports that Sam Didier pushed the team to exaggerate attributes, which produced silhouettes readable at a distance (via summary) [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-torbjrn-became-the-art-inspiration-for-all-of-overwatch).
- **Blizzard, StarCraft II.** This is the top-down RTS case. Lead artist Allen Dilling: "in RTS you have to be more crystal clear, because the top-down view, there's 500 units swarming on-screen… you really have to have a unique silhouette and distinct outline for each guy." (verified) [NAG BlizzCon 2011 interview](https://www.nag.co.za/2011/11/17/blizzcon-2011-exclusive-starcraft-ii-interview/). Design director Dustin Browder said units had to be big, "as big as we dare", and that "Too many units causes confusion." (verified) [Kotaku, GDC 2011 report](https://kotaku.com/the-sacrifices-of-starcraft-ii-made-in-the-name-of-spor-5777029)
- **Supercell, Brawl Stars.** One of the stated creative pillars is "Iconic simplicity". Fernanda Oliveira says "less is more" and that a character should "pick one thing and go with that thing." (verified) [PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/5-creative-pillars-for-designing-brawlers-in-supercells-brawl-stars/)

### Applying it top-down and at small size
- From straight above, the silhouette is the **footprint plus anything that sticks out**, such as barrels, prongs, arms and petals. Height disappears, so a tall spire and a squat dome can produce the same circle. Distinctness has to come from the plan view: the outline's shape class, the number and length of protrusions, symmetry order (2-, 3-, 4-, 5- or 6-fold), and concavities such as notches and holes. This is my inference from the sources above, applied to a plan view.
- League and Dota 2 both say the silhouette should **show facing direction**. For Hollowlight's rotating parts, the aiming element should be asymmetric (a barrel or a prong) so the direction reads from the shape. A glow cannot do this.
- Draw the test at the size players actually see. Microsoft's icon guidance says to design a silhouette that is "distinctive, yet legible at small sizes" and to "use as few shapes with as few corners as possible". (verified) [Microsoft app icon guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/iconography/app-icon-design)
- **Hollowlight consequence.** Orbiting shards, additive halos and rings are radially symmetric "clouds". In silhouette they all turn into roughly the same disc. If high tiers add these, every ward drifts toward the same blob. Tier additions should make the ward's own unique silhouette feature larger, not add a generic halo on top of it.

## 2. Value contrast, the squint test and the greyscale test

- **Value comes before colour.** Valve's Dota guide: "One can argue that value is more important than color to the design and the success of a character because not only is it used to create focal points, it creates the illusion of depth." Further: "The human eye instinctively looks for boundaries between areas of contrast", and "Separating each unique piece on the character into its own value will also help it read as a distinct element." (verified) [Dota 2 Character Art Guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935)
- **The greyscale test is official studio practice.** Dota: "Before adding color, test your value patterns in-game. Be sure the character's features are recognizable and that the most important features draw the eye." Also: "Don't lose focus! Turn your character to greyscale… and recheck the values during and after the coloring process." (verified, same source)
- **The squint test.** Derek Yu, in his pixel-art tutorial, suggests removing colour to check shading. A sprite should still show "a few large clusters of light and dark" when you squint at it. (verified) [Derek Yu, Pixel Art Tutorial](https://derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart.html). Squinting blurs away detail so only the value structure and the focal hierarchy remain (via summary) [Polypane, "Debug your visual hierarchy with the squint test"](https://polypane.app/blog/debug-your-visual-hierarchy-with-the-squint-test/).
- **The most important read gets the most contrast.** TF2 puts the "highest contrast at chest level, where weapon is held". (verified) [GDC 2008 deck](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf). Dota uses a dark-to-light gradient toward the important parts and more value contrast in the upper torso than the lower body. (verified) [Dota guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935). Riot's VFX lead says a focal area usually holds the extreme values (highest contrast), the highest saturation and the most detail (via summary) [Oli McDonald, "Anatomy of a League missile pt 1"](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-1-creating-areas-of-focus/11614).
- **Avoid absolute extremes.** Dota says "Absolute white and black should not be used… becoming flattened or overblown and losing detail." (verified). Riot's VFX style guide also says to avoid 0% black and 100% white and to keep the brightest values for the impact moment (via summary) [VFX Apprentice summary of Riot VFX style guide](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips).
- **Hollowlight consequence.** Additive glows push overlapping pixels toward white. When several wards' halos overlap, they flatten into one bright mass with no value structure. That is exactly the "overblown" failure Dota describes. Keep each ward's body in the mid-to-dark value range so it separates from the dark teal map with a clear value step. Spend the top 10-15% of the value range on one focal point per ward, such as the emitter or the muzzle.

### How much detail versus shape
- Dota: "Detailed areas should comprise a small percentage of the overall character, and be concentrated in areas of importance. The scale of detail is relative to the size of the character in-game. Details that are too small simply become noise. Larger areas with less or no detail read much clearer from game perspective." It also says "areas of rest" let the detailed areas have "a much greater visual impact". To add detail without noise, keep "the values of the detail close to its background". (verified) [Dota guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935)
- TF2: "High frequency detail is omitted where possible." TF2 also avoided modelling the world in an overly complex way because that would "add an unnecessary level of visual noise". (verified) [SIGGRAPH 2007 notes](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)
- Microsoft icons: "only add additional literal detail to the most prominent layer of an icon." (verified) [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/iconography/app-icon-design)
- **Working rule for 36-60 px.** This is a heuristic synthesised from the above, not a sourced number. The large shape and its value blocks should carry identity. Detail should be about 2-3 small features at most, all of them in the focal zone and none narrower than about 2 px.

## 3. Visual hierarchy and the noise budget

- **Primary, secondary and tertiary reads.**
  - TF2 reads team, then class, then weapon (verified) [GDC 2008](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf).
  - Riot's VFX style guide asks every effect to "clearly define areas of focus with primary and secondary elements" (via summary) [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips).
  - Riot's VFX lead says the primary element is the part that communicates gameplay. "Any part of the missile that is there only to reinforce the thematic should be a Secondary Element." (verified) [realtimevfx, Riot Beardilocks](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-1-creating-areas-of-focus/11614).
  - Riot's VFX update priorities rank (1) gameplay issues, (2) "improving gameplay readability, reducing overall visual noise", (3) thematic resonance (via summary) [Riot, "Behind the scenes of VFX updates"](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-behind-the-scenes-of-vfx-updates/).
- **Visual impact should match gameplay impact.** Riot's "Clarity in League": "the amount of attention an ability grabs should match its level of importance". "Visual noise adds up quickly and can make it difficult to react properly in teamfights." "Flashiness can feel great… but it comes at a cost." "We'll never turn everything up to 11." Riot also wants to avoid "near-permanent transformations", meaning always-on flashy states. (verified) [Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)
- **Effects stay short.** Riot's VFX guide argues that long-lasting effects clutter the screen and hide targets, and warns "Don't stack or layer too many alpha shapes" (via summary) [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips). The four goals of the original guide are clarity of gameplay, minimising clutter, promoting theme, and delight. The original PDF has been taken down, and [the nexus URL](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/) now redirects (via summary; noted on [realtimevfx](https://realtimevfx.com/t/official-league-of-legends-vfx-style-guide/3392)).
- **Saturation budget.** Riot gives environment art the lowest saturation, UI a controlled range, and VFX the highest ceiling so it cuts through (via summary) [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips). TF2 uses "muted colors dominating and small areas of saturation to give further visual interest" (verified) [SIGGRAPH 2007](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf). Dota: "Choose very small areas for the highest saturation… Large areas of high saturation overwhelm the viewer" (verified) [Dota guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935).
- **StarCraft II.** Units were made as big "as we dare", and the unit roster was kept small because "too many units causes confusion" (verified) [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-sacrifices-of-starcraft-ii-made-in-the-name-of-spor-5777029). Team colour is the identity channel layered on top, and it visibly tints whole units: "If we set the team color to purple they're a bit more purple and brown" (Samwise Didier, via summary) [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/sc2-week-brand-new-zerg-art-style-revealed/). A Blizzard art panel credits "strong silhouettes, exaggerated proportions, distinctive animations, and bold, saturated colors" (unverified; GameSpot blocked the fetch) [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/blizzard-talks-starcraft-ii-art-design/1100-6171176/).
- **Supercell.** I found no public Supercell art style guide. Their stated pillar is "Iconic simplicity" and "pick one thing" (verified) [PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/5-creative-pillars-for-designing-brawlers-in-supercells-brawl-stars/). Their UI job postings ask artists to improve "readability" (via summary) [Supercell careers](https://supercell.com/en/careers/senior-ui-artist/66476c5d-30b6-4772-aa41-94d251e3d73c/).

### A noise budget for Hollowlight (synthesis)
On a board with 20-40 wards, the screen-level hierarchy should be:
1. **Primary:** threats (enemies, the leak path) and the attack that is firing right now.
2. **Secondary:** ward identity (silhouette and body value).
3. **Tertiary:** ward tier and idle flavour.

Idle glows and orbiters are tertiary, so they should be the quietest layer: low alpha, low saturation, no additive white cores. Bright additive light belongs only to the attack moment, which is short and tied to gameplay. This follows Riot's "importance = attention" rule.

## 4. Shape language and Gestalt

- **Shape connotations.** The conventional character-design mapping is:
  - circle: friendly or soft
  - square: stable, sturdy, defensive
  - triangle: dynamic, dangerous, aggressive

  This is a widely taught rule of thumb from Disney and Pixar practice, not a law (via summary) [Dream Farm Studios](https://dreamfarmstudios.com/shape-language-in-character-design/).

  There is experimental support for angular shapes reading as threatening:
  - Bar & Neta (2006) found people prefer curved objects to sharp-angled versions of the same objects, and proposed that sharp contours convey threat (via summary) [Bar & Neta PDF](https://moshebar.org/_files/ugd/a77db7_5598c791a95a4436960eb2fe671ca1e5.pdf).
  - A 2023 study reports that sharper angles are judged more threatening (via summary) [Psychological Research, 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-023-01822-y).
  - Later work questions whether threat fully explains the curvature preference (via summary, same search).
- **Picking distinct base shapes for many unit types.** This is a synthesis. The key is to use independent shape dimensions and combine them, because a few dimensions multiply into many recognisable classes:
  - **Outline class:** round, polygonal (count the corners: tri, square, hex), star or spiky, crescent or notched, ring with a hole.
  - **Symmetry and protrusion count:** 1 barrel, 2 prongs, 3 arms, 4 petals, 6 spokes. Counts of 4 or fewer are read instantly. [Subitizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subitizing) lets people perceive "four or fewer items" without counting (verified, Kaufman et al. 1949, via Wikipedia). Stephen Few notes that numerosity is only reliable "if quantities don't exceed three" (verified) [Perceptual Edge](https://www.perceptualedge.com/blog/?p=2957).
  - **Aspect ratio:** compact versus elongated.
  - **Edge character:** smooth curves versus hard angles.

  Five outline classes × four protrusion counts already gives 20 combinations, before colour is used at all. Group related wards into families that share an outline class, the way TF2's red team uses "angular geometry" and blue uses "orthogonal forms" to separate the bases (verified) [SIGGRAPH 2007](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf).
- **Gestalt similarity works against Hollowlight right now.** "Items which share a visual characteristic are perceived as more related than items that are dissimilar", and "the common color tends to stand out more prominently than other traits, such as shape" (verified) [NN/g, Similarity Principle](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-similarity/). If many wards share cyan glows and orbiting shards, viewers group them as one kind of thing, which is the opposite of what is wanted. Shared motifs should be reserved for wards that really belong to the same family.
- **Feature hierarchy: hue masks shape.** Callaghan showed that "the visual system favors color over shape". Background variations in colour slowed the detection of shape patterns, while random variation in shape did not affect colour detection. Strong features such as luminance and hue "can mask weaker features like curvature". Healey & Enns conclude that "the most important data attributes should be displayed with the most salient visual features". (verified) [Healey & Enns 2012, IEEE TVCG](https://healey.csc.ncsu.edu/publications/15783.pdf). **Implication for Hollowlight:** random-looking hue variation from multicoloured glows actively hides the silhouette differences you are relying on. Either make hue consistent within a ward, or make hue carry the most important category.
- **Figure-ground and closure.** Outlines "help separate objects from the background, as well as other objects" (verified) [Lospec, Pixel Art Outlines](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines). TF2 achieves the same separation with rim light instead of dark lines (verified) [SIGGRAPH 2007](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf).

## 5. Colour

- **Do not rely on hue alone.** Game Accessibility Guidelines: "use colour as a back-up for another means of communicating the information". Red/green deficiency affects "around 8-10% of males". Test with a simulator such as Color Oracle, and design for 100% severity. (verified) [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-colour-alone/). AbleGamers' Accessible Player Experiences recommends pairing symbols with colours, giving examples such as Two Dots and ReCore (verified) [AbleGamers APX "Distinguish This From That"](https://accessible.games/accessible-player-experiences/access-patterns/distinguish-this-from-that/).
- **Confusion pairs that hit Hollowlight's palette directly.** For red-green colour blindness (protan and deutan), Wikipedia lists confusable pairs including **cyan and grey**, **blue and purple**, yellow and neon green, and red/green/orange/brown. For tritan deficiency it lists blue and green, and violet and yellow-green. Prevalence is about 8% of men of Northern European descent. (verified) [Wikipedia: Color blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_blindness). **Implication:** cyan-versus-violet glows, the core of the current palette, are at risk of collapsing for protan and deutan players. Cyan halos may also read as grey against a dark teal map.
- **How many hues can be told apart at a glance.** Healey (1996) found detection was "rapid and accurate for all colours" in 3- and 5-colour displays, but mixed at 7 and 9. He concluded that "seven isoluminant colours is the maximum we can display at one time, while still allowing rapid and accurate identification", and only if the colours are chosen carefully for colour distance and named-category separation. He also quotes a military guideline of "no more than five to seven colours at a time". (verified) [Healey 1996, UBC TR-96-10](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1996/TR-96-10_0.pdf). A rule of about 12 hues or fewer for nominal colour coding is attributed to Ware's *Information Visualization* (unverified; seen only in secondary slides) [UBC lecture slides](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~tmm/talks/sig03.multimodal/mgp00025.txt). Few: "Colors become difficult to discriminate when objects are tiny" (verified) [Perceptual Edge](https://www.perceptualedge.com/blog/?p=2957).

  **For 20+ wards:** hue cannot be the identity key. Use at most about 5-6 hue families as a category channel (for example, damage type or ward family) and let silhouette carry the individual identity.
- **Reserve saturation for gameplay.** Small saturated areas on a muted base (TF2, Dota, as above). Dota also says "Avoid using colors which are dominant in the Dota map" (verified) [Dota guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935). **Hollowlight consequence:** teal and green ward bodies or glows will sink into the map, so keep ward hues away from the map's hue, or separate them strongly by value.
- **Complementary hues.** Riot's guidance is that one hue should dominate and the complement should be a desaturated accent (via summary) [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips). Dota warns that complementary colours "vibrate" and compete for attention (verified) [Dota guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935).
- **Effects versus units.** If ward idle effects use the same saturation and brightness as attacks and enemy telegraphs, the attention hierarchy flattens. Riot's rule "the amount of attention an ability grabs should match its level of importance" applies here (verified) [Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/).

## 6. Showing level and upgrade at small size

- **Pips and counts.** Use 1-4 discrete marks. Subitizing covers "four or fewer items" (verified) [Wikipedia: Subitizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subitizing), and Few says numerosity is reliable up to about 3 (verified) [Perceptual Edge](https://www.perceptualedge.com/blog/?p=2957). Pips must sit in a consistent location on every ward, for example a fixed arc at the bottom rim, so the eye learns where to look. That consistency is a synthesis, not a sourced rule. Pips should be solid, at least about 3 px, and contrast in value with both the ward body and the map.
- **Change the silhouette at milestone tiers, not every tier.** Clash of Clans defences grow in size almost every level, but make significant visual changes only at certain levels. The Archer Tower changes at levels 6, 11 and 14 and gains a third archer at level 7. (via summary; fan wiki) [clasher.us Archer Tower](https://www.clasher.us/clash-of-clans/unit/Archer_Tower). BTD6's per-tier appearance changes are well known in play, but I could not verify them from a source (unverified).
- **Size growth.** Size is a preattentive feature (verified as a listed feature) [Healey & Enns 2012](https://healey.csc.ncsu.edu/publications/15783.pdf), and StarCraft II deliberately made units "as big as we dare" for readability (verified) [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-sacrifices-of-starcraft-ii-made-in-the-name-of-spor-5777029). On a tile grid, though, growth is capped by the footprint. Use small steps, for example +6-10% radius per tier, and let the protrusions grow (longer barrel, more prongs) rather than the core.
- **A power read via material and value.** Show the tier by escalating within the ward's own identity: stone, then metal, then crystal, or dim, then bright focal core. Riot's skin rule is that the "primary feature should never be removed or significantly altered", so recognisability survives the change (verified) [Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/). Upgrades should therefore amplify the unique feature, never cover it with a generic aura.
- **Ornament, frames and rims.** One consistent frame language works across all wards, such as a plain rim, then a notched rim, then a crowned rim. It is cheap and it scales to 20+ wards, because it is one system rather than 20 bespoke ones. This is a synthesis. Microsoft notes that a consistent grid and rim language makes a set of icons "balance well" together (verified, general) [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/iconography/app-icon-design).
- **Motion as an identity channel.** Motion (flicker, direction, velocity) is a studied preattentive channel. "Rapid changes will produce motion transients that can alert viewers." (verified) [Healey & Enns 2012](https://healey.csc.ncsu.edu/publications/15783.pdf). Blizzard credits "distinctive animations" as one of its readability tools (unverified, GameSpot) [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/blizzard-talks-starcraft-ii-art-design/1100-6171176/). Riot's VFX timing guidance uses a non-linear life of anticipation, then early peak, then dissipation, and warns that "linear timing curves ruin gameplay immersion" (via summary) [VFX Apprentice](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips).

  For Hollowlight, give each ward family a distinct idle rhythm: slow rotation, a pulse, a stepped tick, or a bob. Give each ward a short anticipation pose before firing. Note the risk: constant motion on every ward is also noise, and Riot avoids "near-permanent" flashy states. Keep idle motion low-amplitude.

## 7. Advice specific to top-down and small scale

- **Design and test at the real size.** Octicons: "Always design two versions of each icon: a 16px version and a 24px version" (verified) [GitHub Primer Octicons guidelines](https://primer.style/octicons/design-guidelines). Derek Yu: "The smaller a sprite gets, the less your sprite looks like what it's supposed to represent" (verified) [Derek Yu](https://derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart.html). For Hollowlight, review every ward at 36 px, not only at 60 px.
- **Avoid thin lines and extreme thick/thin mixes.** Microsoft says a balanced silhouette "avoids extremes of thick and thin shapes", and recommends using "as few shapes with as few corners as possible" and keeping gradients to "one or two steps" (verified) [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/iconography/app-icon-design). Octicons aligns "the outer edge of shapes to pixel boundaries when possible" (verified) [Octicons](https://primer.style/octicons/design-guidelines). On canvas, a 1 px anti-aliased stroke on a half pixel renders as a grey smear, so prefer strokes of at least 1.5-2 px and snap coordinates (practitioner consensus, via summary) [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/icon-design-principles/).
- **Keep the light direction consistent.** Microsoft's icon gradients hint at "an ambient light angle coming from the top left", with lighter hues on the top left for consistency (verified) [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/iconography/app-icon-design). Derek Yu uses a single light source with bright up-facing surfaces and dark bottoms (verified) [Derek Yu](https://derekyu.com/makegames/pixelart.html). For top-down vector wards, use one fixed screen-space light, for example from the top left, for the body bevel. **Do not rotate the bevel with the aiming turret.** Rotating highlights read as flicker.
- **Use outlines or rims to separate wards from the map.** A dark outline, or TF2's rim light, separates a sprite from the background and from neighbours (verified) [Lospec](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines) and [SIGGRAPH 2007](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf). Lospec also notes that an outline can make an object look like it is floating where it touches the ground (verified). On a dark teal map, a darker-than-map outline plus a lighter body edge, a "double edge", works in both value directions (synthesis).
- **Keep transparency layers few.** Microsoft: "Use as few layers as possible" (verified). Riot: "Don't stack or layer too many alpha shapes" (via summary). These apply directly to stacked additive halos.
- **Check in context.** Dota tells artists to place the hero across both sides of the map in day and night (verified) [Dota guide](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935). For Hollowlight, test wards on the map's lightest and darkest tiles, with path and enemies present, mid-wave.

## A readability checklist for Hollowlight wards

Each check is meant to be pass/fail, run on a contact sheet that renders every ward at every tier. Render the sheet at actual size, at 36 px and at 48 px, over the real map background.

1. **Silhouette test.** Fill every ward at tier 1 as a solid black shape at 36 px. A tester shown random silhouettes names the correct ward ≥90% of the time, and no two wards are confused with each other more than once in 20 trials.
2. **Silhouette is kept across tiers.** In silhouette, each ward's top tier is still matched to its own tier 1 rather than to another ward ≥90% of the time. Riot's "primary feature never removed" rule.
3. **Greyscale test.** With the screen desaturated (canvas `filter: grayscale(1)`), every ward is still identifiable, and its focal point (emitter or muzzle) is the brightest region of the ward.
4. **Value separation from the map.** Every ward body differs in luminance from both the lightest and darkest map tiles by a contrast ratio of ≥3:1, either across its edge or through its outline. This adapts Microsoft's 3:1 icon target.
5. **One focal point.** When squinting or blurred (for example a 4 px Gaussian blur), each ward shows one clear brightest spot and at most 3 value blocks. No ward has two competing bright zones.
6. **No idle white-out.** With 10 wards of mixed types placed adjacent and idle, no screen pixel between them reaches near-white (L* > 90) from overlapping glows. Near-white is reserved for firing and impact moments.
7. **Hue budget.** Ward identity uses at most 6 hue families across the whole roster, each mapped to a documented category (family or damage type). No ward's identity depends on hue alone.
8. **Colour-blind pass.** Run the contact sheet through a deuteranopia and protanopia simulator (Color Oracle or a canvas filter). Every ward and every tier is still distinguishable. Pay particular attention to cyan versus grey and blue versus violet.
9. **Map-hue avoidance.** No ward's dominant body colour sits within the map's teal-green hue band unless it is separated by at least about 25 L* in value.
10. **Facing read.** For every rotating ward, the aim direction can be read from the silhouette alone, with glow and colour removed.
11. **Tier is readable at a glance.** The tier is shown by 1-4 solid pips (≥3 px, consistent position across all wards) and at least one shape change at milestone tiers. A tester can state the tier of any ward at 36 px within 1 second, ≥90% correct.
12. **Tier changes add to the identity feature, not a generic aura.** At least half of each tier's visual change is to the ward's unique feature (longer barrel, more prongs, a bigger crystal), and at most one shared tier motif is used roster-wide.
13. **Minimum feature size.** No stroke narrower than 1.5 px and no meaningful gap or feature smaller than 3 px at 36 px render size. Coordinates are snapped so key edges are not half-pixel smears.
14. **Motion budget.** Idle animation amplitude stays below the attack animation. Every ward family has a distinct idle rhythm. Every ward has a pre-fire anticipation of ≥100 ms that is distinguishable from its idle motion.
15. **Crowd test.** In a mid-wave screenshot with 25+ wards and enemies, a tester can point to every instance of a named ward type within 3 seconds, and enemies and attack telegraphs remain the most salient elements on screen.

**Note on the numbers.** The thresholds in checks 1, 4-6, 9 and 11-15 are proposed starting values that I synthesised from the sources above. They are not published standards, so tune them in playtests.
