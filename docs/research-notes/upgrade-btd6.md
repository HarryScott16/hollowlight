# Bloons TD 6: how towers change appearance with upgrades (BTD5 for contrast)

## Sources

The main wikis (bloons.fandom.com, bloonswiki.com) blocked automated fetching, as did Reddit, TV Tropes and the Epic Games Store article. Most wiki facts below come from search summaries of those pages, so they are second-hand. Claims the agent could not verify are marked (unverified).

## 1. What changes at each tier

**Tiers 1-2: small accessory or colour changes on the same body.**
- Ninja's Sharp Shurikens (tier 2): the suit turns black, and the headband and belt turn red. Crosspathed versions usually change only the headband and belt colour (bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Sharp_Shurikens_(BTD6), bloonswiki.com/Sharp_Shurikens_(BTD6)).
- General pattern: headgear, goggles, a band, or a bigger or recoloured weapon. The silhouette stays the same (unverified as a general rule).

**Tier 3: the biggest jump, where the tower gets a new identity.** The weapon, costume or whole body is swapped.
- Dart Monkey:
  - Spike-o-pult replaces the dart with a wooden catapult on wheels, run by a plain Dart Monkey (bloonswiki.com/Spike-o-pult_(BTD6)).
  - Crossbow swaps the dart for a crossbow.
- Ninja: Double Shot gains a white hood. Bloonjitsu (tier 4) swaps it for a woven rice hat (bloonswiki.com/Bloonjitsu_(BTD6)).
- Super Monkey:
  - Sun Avatar: gold cape and suit, a headdress, an orange eye band and glowing white eyes (bloonswiki.com/Sun_Avatar_(BTD6)).
  - Dark Knight starts the dark-armour line.
  - Robo Monkey adds robotic parts (robotic parts unverified).
- Boomerang: Bionic Boomerang replaces an arm with a mechanical one (bloonswiki.com/Bionic_Boomerang_(BTD6)).

**Tier 4: a stronger version of the tier 3 idea.** It is bigger and more ornate, often with glow or armour.
- Sun Temple replaces the monkey with a two-level ceramic temple (bloonswiki.com/Sun_Temple_(BTD6)).
- Juggernaut has a bigger catapult and a bigger ball (unverified).

**Tier 5: a dramatic transformation.**
- Wizard Lord Phoenix: the hat goes, and the robe gains a cowl with eyes, a feathered crest and a beak (bloonswiki.com/Wizard_Lord).
- Absolute Zero: a blue tribal mask with glowing eye holes (bloonswiki.com/Ice_Monkey_(BTD6)).
- Avatar of Wrath: almost all red, with a black wolf hood and cape (bloonswiki.com/Avatar_of_Wrath_(BTD6)).
- Glaive Lord: two glaives orbit the monkey (bloonswiki.com/Glaive_Lord_(BTD6)).
- Vengeful True Sun God: a dark metal body with red lines, deliberately borrowing looks from other tier 5s (bloonswiki.com/True_Sun_God_(BTD6)).

**Paragons (a stage beyond tier 5):**
- Each Paragon merges cues from all three tier 5s into one design.
- Its look changes every 20 "degrees" (its power level). Ascended Shadow, for example, reveals a growing flame eye and later gains horns and orbiting talismans (bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Paragons, /Ascended_Shadow, /Ninja_Monkey_Paragon).

## 2. How the three paths diverge

- **Tiers 1-2:** the paths differ only by small cues (unverified as a rule).
- **From tier 3:** the paths are clearly different, and each path takes one kind of change:
  - Dart Monkey: a machine (catapult), a crowd theme (fan club), or a weapon swap (crossbow).
  - Super Monkey: a divine path (gold and white), a tech path (metal, red eyes) and a dark path (black armour).
  - Druid: storm, jungle or wrath, with wrath shown by an all-red recolour.
- **Each path has its own colour family** (gold for Sun, red/black for Wrath, dark for Night, metal for Tech), and later merged designs reuse these colours.

## 3. How a tower stays recognisable

- **The same monkey body throughout:** the same fur, face, eyes and pose.
- **One signature item per tower that is upgraded, never removed:** the Engineer's hard hat, the Sniper's beret, the Wizard's robe and hat, the Ninja's headband.
- **A visible operator:** when the body becomes a machine (catapult, temple, trap), a plain monkey often stays visible as its operator.

## 4. How the secondary path shows

- **At low tiers:** lightly, as a partial accessory or colour swap.
- **From tier 3:** the main path's model takes over.
- **At tier 5:** a crosspath rarely changes the look. Glaive Lord is one exception: its glaives turn red-orange with Red Hot Rangs (bloonswiki.com/Glaive_Lord_(BTD6)).

## 5. Projectiles and effects

- **Projectiles change along with the model:** darts become spiked balls, then giant splitting balls; glaives become red-hot; Super Monkey's darts become lasers, plasma and sunbeams. Summons (phoenixes) appear at the top tiers.
- **Effects can be drawn bigger than the real hit area,** as with the Tack Shooter Paragon (bloonswiki.com/Crucible_of_Steel_and_Flame).
- **The agent's estimate (not sourced):**
  - At tiers 1-2, nearly all the impressiveness is in the model.
  - At tier 5, roughly half comes from effects, orbiting objects, auras and summons.

## 6. Size

- **The footprint never changes on upgrade**, even when the model grows (bloonswiki.com/Footprint).
- **Growth goes into** height, props that reach outward, and effects.

## 7. Design reasoning

- **No Ninja Kiwi statement was found.**
- **BTD5 contrast:** BTD5 had 2 paths of 4 tiers, drawn as 2D sprites with more literal designs.
- **The agent's own reading:**
  - Cheap tiers keep the silhouette, so a busy map stays readable.
  - Tier 3 is the first big purchase, so it gets the first big redesign.
  - Tier 5s work as trophies.

## Principles the agent drew from this

1. Keep the core body, palette and pose fixed. Upgrades add to the core or wrap around it.
2. Give each ward one signature item that every path upgrades and none removes.
3. Tiers 1-2: one small accessory or colour cue per tier, with the silhouette unchanged.
4. Tier 3: the first identity change, by swapping the weapon or costume or bringing in a machine. The paths are clearly distinct from here.
5. Each path keeps one kind of change and one colour family through tiers 3-5.
6. Tier 4: the tier 3 idea, scaled up and ornamented.
7. Tier 5: a new silhouette plus one signature effect (orbiters, aura, summon). Glowing eyes and new colours are reliable "final form" signals.
8. Show the secondary path lightly, if at all.
9. Projectiles progress along with the model.
10. Grow upward and outward visually, but keep the footprint fixed.
11. A machine or building form keeps a small figure of the ward visible as its operator.
12. A later "beyond tier 5" stage could merge cues from all three paths.
