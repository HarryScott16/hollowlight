# Unifying gimmicks and readability systems for Hollowlight wards

Research notes for Hollowlight (wards vs. the Gloom, defending the Heartfire). Today there are 8 wards with no shared concept (crossbow, mortar, ice crystal, tesla coil, rifle, flamethrower brazier, light prism, lamp-post aura). The plan is 20+.

Every factual claim has a source URL next to it. "(unverified)" marks things I believe but could not confirm from a fetched source. Where only a secondary summary was available, I say so.

---

## Topic A: Unifying gimmicks

### A1. The pattern: a fixed base template plus one variable that carries the role

Across the examples below, the same structure keeps appearing:

1. A **base template** that every unit shares, such as a monkey, a plant with a face, a Pikmin body, Kirby, or an Olympian god. The template makes the whole roster read as one family ("this is one of mine") and carries the charm.
2. One **variable slot** that carries the role, such as the gear held, the plant species, the colour, the hat, or the god's element.
3. **Borrowed real-world knowledge**, so that the variable explains itself. Peas get shot, nuts block, fire burns, blue swims.

#### Plants vs. Zombies (PopCap, George Fan)

- **Why plants:** they stay still, and stationary units make good towers. Mark Rosewater (Magic's head designer) recounts Fan telling him: "I chose plants because I needed units that would stay stationary." Zombies were chosen because they are slow, which gives the player time to set up. — https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/piggybacking-2012-08-20
- **"Piggybacking":** Rosewater's name for this is "building design around an already known and understood concept". Players already know that plants grow and zombies shamble, so the rules feel obvious. He calls the zombie choice "chunking", meaning several traits (many of them, dim, slow) are packed into one familiar idea. — same URL
- **Visuals explain function:** in Fan's GDC 2012 talk (10 tutorial tips), one tip is that each character's look should signal its role. The Peashooter's oversized mouth signals a projectile, and the shielding and energy plants follow the same rule. He also sets a cap of "a maximum of eight words on the screen at any given moment". — https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan
- **Charm comes from faces:** Fan says plants "can give them a lot of personality", "I wanted all the characters to seem like more than just plants", and that adding blinking "really sold it". Lead artist Rich Werner on the Wall-nut: "His big goofy eyes are awesome… you feel for the guy when he starts getting eaten." Early builds had the Wall-nut shed tears as it was eaten. On mechanics plus art: "if you can have an amazing mechanic with stellar art, it's like Wonder Twin Powers." — The Escapist, "The Sunny Side of the Zombiepocalypse" (read via mirror https://escapistmagazines.pages.dev/posts/the-sunny-side-of-the-zombiepocalypse ; original https://www.escapistmagazine.com/?p=53331)
- **Gameplay first, character second:** a later PvZ design director described adopting Nintendo's approach of designing the mechanic first and then the character, so the visual shows what the unit does. This comes from a search-result summary of an EA blog post that now returns 404 (https://www.ea.com/news/september-monthly-update), so treat it as **(unverified)**.
- **Where it strains:** players on EA's PvZ 2 forums complain that newer plants are near-clones of existing ones and ask for original mechanics instead of number bumps. — https://forums.ea.com/discussions/plants-vs-zombies-2-en/improving-plant-versatility-stop-the-clones-and-buff-classic-plants/13241276 (the page returned 403 to me, so this is from the search snippet; player opinion, not analysis)

**Takeaway for Hollowlight:** the template should answer a mechanical question as well as an aesthetic one. Plants were chosen because they don't move, which is a tower's defining trait. For Hollowlight the natural mechanical fact is that every ward is a **light source** that pushes back darkness.

#### Bloons TD 6 (Ninja Kiwi): every tower is a monkey with different gear

- 23–25 towers in four categories: **Primary** (Dart, Boomerang, Bomb, Tack, Ice, Glue, Desperado), **Military** (Sniper, Sub, Buccaneer, Ace, Heli, Mortar, Dartling), **Magic** (Wizard, Super, Ninja, Alchemist, Druid, Mermonkey) and **Support** (Spike Factory, Village, Banana Farm, Engineer). Heroes sit outside the categories. — https://www.bloonswiki.com/Tower_category (403 to me; contents from search snippets) and https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Bloons_TD_6
- Giant Bomb notes that the **category is shown by the colour of the tower's icon** in the shop. — https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Bloons_TD_6 . The exact colours are **(unverified)**; from memory, Primary is blue/teal, Military green, Magic purple and Support orange/brown.
- The monkey is the template and the gear is the variable: a dart, a bomb cannon, a sniper rifle, a wizard robe, a farm. The name reads "<Gear> Monkey", and the gear itself is the role icon.
- The monkey origin is anecdote only. CEO Rob Walker: "Legend has it that Stephen's wife Roseanne suggested that monkeys should be involved." — https://store.epicgames.com/news/inside-bloons-the-multimillion-dollar-tower-defense-empire-built-on-monkeys
- Upgrades show up in the model, not just the number. The Tack/Blade Shooter's path-1 crosspaths "each add another blade into its emblem", and its path-3 crosspaths add teeth to the blade emblem and one more blade per layer for each upgrade. — https://bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_Shooter_(BTD6) (search snippet)
- **Paragons** (added in v27) merge "design elements from all three upgrade paths" of a tower into one ultimate form that absorbs the other monkeys of its type. — https://www.bloonswiki.com/Paragons (search snippet)
- **Where it strains:** with the monkey fixed, newer towers lean on increasingly specific gear (Mermonkey, Beast Handler, Desperado). Ninja is listed under Magic in some sources and Military in others (Giant Bomb vs. wiki templates), which suggests some towers sit between categories.

#### Pikmin (Nintendo): colour is the ability

- All Pikmin share one body (a leaf, flower or bud on the head, a small body). Colour carries the ability. **Red** resists fire and is the strongest fighter, **yellow** resists electricity and can be thrown higher, and **blue** survives water. Pikmin 4 adds Ice ("can freeze water and enemies"). Rock, Winged, White, Purple and Glow also exist. — https://primagames.com/news/pikmin-3-pikmin-types ; https://nc.inverse.com/gaming/pikmin-4-find-get-red-yellow-ice-blue-purple-rock-winged-glow-white
- Abilities have drifted between games. Yellows carried bomb rocks in Pikmin 1 and dig faster in 3 and 4. The colour stayed fixed while the specifics changed. — https://www.pikminwiki.com/Pikmin_seed (search snippet)
- **Where it strains:** after the primary colours, newer types need a second differentiator besides hue (Rock is a stone body, Winged has wings, Purple is fat). Colour alone ran out after about 5 types (my observation; **unverified** as a designer statement).

#### Kirby copy abilities (HAL): hat is the ability

- From Kirby Super Star onward, "Every time Kirby absorbs an ability, he wears a unique 'hat' that indicates what power he has". The hats grew out of the Helper system. — https://gameranx.com/updates/id/372692/article/kirby-super-star-origins-revealed-by-sakurai-in-newest-video/
- **This is the cleanest "one change" example:** the round pink body never changes, and only the headwear varies.
- **Where it breaks:** director Shinya Kumazaki on Planet Robobot: "once you go over 25 copy abilities, you start to see an effect on the frequency of each ability per stage and on the balancing", and "we're always struggling to balance out the old and new ones." — https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2016/09/kirby_planet_robobot_director_discusses_how_copy_abilities_are_chosen
- Sakurai reportedly found the hats "troublesome" in Smash because "Kirby has a lot of hats in that game!" In other words, a template plus a variable multiplies production cost. — https://gameranx.com/updates/id/372692/article/kirby-super-star-origins-revealed-by-sakurai-in-newest-video/

#### Hades (Supergiant): boons by god

- Each Olympian's boons share one mechanical theme: Zeus is lightning, Poseidon is knockback waves, Demeter is chill. The god is the template and the god's domain is the variable. — https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game) (search snippet)
- **How Supergiant adds a member:** for Demeter, Kasavin's first idea ("grasping vines slowing enemies down") was "Nothing too inspiring". They went back to the myth and landed on an "ice queen". Designer Gavin Simon Lai describes the test as "what would feel cool and distinct", and ideas that are "too same-y or too complicated" get cut. — https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-added-new-goddess-demeter-to-i-hades-i-pantheon
- **Lesson:** a new member has to be distinct inside the shared frame and also come out of the fiction, not just fill a stat gap.

#### Element TD: element is the role

- Six elements, each with a fixed attack personality. **Light** is long-range single-target with no splash. **Dark** hits hardest per shot but is "very sluggish". **Water** has the smallest splash and the fastest attacks. **Fire** has the largest splash and the shortest range. **Nature** sits in the middle. **Earth** is medium splash with more punch per hit. Matchups form a cycle: Light beats Dark beats Water beats Fire beats Nature beats Earth beats Light (2x / 0.5x damage). — https://forums.eletd.com/topic/2681-characteristics-of-the-elemental-towers/
- Dual and triple towers combine elements, so a small base vocabulary of 6 produces dozens of towers. Each combined tower inherits a readable parent. — same URL
- **Lesson:** the element sets the attack shape (range versus splash versus speed), not just the damage colour. In Element TD, Light is the long-range sniper.

#### Pokémon types: colour code shared across 1,000+ creatures

- 18 types, each with a fixed colour used across UI badges and moves. Fan wikis enforce this with templates such as `{{Pokecolor}}`, which outputs a fixed hex per type. — https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Template:Pokecolor ; https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Help_talk:Color_templates
- One fan site extracted three shades per type: a "medium" one for the type symbol, a "dark" one for the type name, and a "light" one for attacks of that type. That separates decorative colour from text colour. — https://v11.pokemonaaah.net/?p=603 (search snippet)
- **Where it strains:** past 1,000 creatures, fans repeatedly say the designers are "running out of ideas", and object-based Pokémon draw particular ire. Takato Utsunomiya (Pokémon Company COO) said that if they ever lose the ability to generate new ideas, continuing would be difficult. — https://www.ggrecon.com/articles/the-pokemon-company-tackles-quantity-over-quality-complaints/ ; https://kotaku.com/pokemon-designs-arent-getting-worse-they-may-be-gettin-5969098 (search snippets)

#### Slime Rancher: composable traits

- Feed a slime another slime's plort and it becomes a Largo: larger, with both slimes' physical characteristics combined, producing both plorts. — https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Monomi_Park (search snippet)
- **Lesson:** if each base unit has one visual signature, hybrids or upgrades can be read as "A's body plus B's feature". This works only because each base slime has exactly one strong signature (cat ears, rock spikes, and so on). The specific features are **(unverified)**.

#### Not researched in depth

Vampire Survivors, Hollow Knight charms, Cult of the Lamb, Spiritfarer, Overcooked and Moonlighter were cut for budget. I found no designer statements on them in the time available.

### A2. Why gimmicks work

| Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Recognition is free:** the template says "friendly unit", so the eye only has to parse the variable. | Kirby hats (one body, swap a hat); BTD monkeys; Riot says a good silhouette has "a defining primary characteristic that's unique to that champion" — https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/ |
| **Piggybacking:** the variable borrows meaning players already have. | Rosewater on PvZ — https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/piggybacking-2012-08-20 |
| **Charm:** faces and eyes create attachment and humour, which make losses feel bad in a fun way. | Werner on the Wall-nut being eaten — Escapist (above) |
| **Teaching:** if the look explains the function, tooltips can stay under 8 words. | Fan GDC 2012 — https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan |
| **Productive constraint:** a frame forces designers away from defaults. | Rosewater, "Restrictions breed creativity" (lesson in his GDC talk *20 Years, 20 Lessons Learned*): "Placing restrictions on yourself, leads you to places you would never naturally go." — https://elitegamedevelopers.substack.com/p/magic-the-gathering-twenty-years-twenty-lessons-learned (secondary summary) |
| **Cohesion against an enemy:** a shared template makes "us vs. them" legible. | StarCraft: Samwise Didier says "It's very easy to tell these races apart, because they are so diametrically opposed." — https://www.engadget.com/2011/10/25/how-blizzard-makes-the-starcraft-2-races-so-iconic/ |

### A3. Where gimmicks break down

1. **Variant exhaustion:** about 25 is a real ceiling for one axis of variation. Kumazaki saw balance and frequency effects past 25 copy abilities (link above). Pokémon past 1,000 draws "out of ideas" complaints (link above). PvZ 2 players complain about clones (link above).
2. **One axis is not enough at scale:** Pikmin needed shape changes on top of colour, and Hearthstone needed lighter Hunter green once Demon Hunter green arrived (see B5).
3. **The template eats the silhouette:** when every unit is the same body, the body stops helping tell units apart. Riot admits Lee Sin "doesn't really have a distinct character model silhouette" and leans on animation instead. — https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/ask-riot-let-s-talk-clarity/ (search snippet). Same-body rosters need the variable part to be large and high-contrast, which is why BTD gear is oversized.
4. **Production multiplication:** every new template feature has to be drawn for every variant (Sakurai and the hats in Smash, link above).
5. **Category ambiguity:** BTD's Ninja is listed under different categories by different sources, which suggests a unit can sit between families. Decide each ward's family once and keep it.

### A4. Candidate gimmicks for Hollowlight (synthesis, not sourced)

The aim is a template that is mechanical (wards are light sources) as well as charming:

- **"Every ward is a lantern."** The template is a lantern housing with a living flame-spirit inside (a face in the flame, PvZ-style). The variable is the housing or apparatus the flame drives: a crossbow cage, a mortar bowl, a frost-glass globe, a coil, a long barrel, an open censer, a prism lens, a street-lamp. The flame's colour is the damage family. Tiers make the flame bigger and brighter. This maps 1:1 onto the current 8 wards.
- **"Every ward is a keeper."** Small hooded lamplighter figures with different tools (the BTD template). There is more personality, but it costs more art and gives the template less to do mechanically.
- **Recommendation:** the lantern-spirit option, because "light pushes back the Gloom" turns the template into a mechanic. Range can literally be the light radius. Support or aura wards can visibly brighten nearby wards' flames.

## Topic B: Systems that keep 20–50+ units distinct

### B1. Bloons TD 6: category colour, three paths, five tiers

- **Four categories** with category-coloured shop icons (link above). Categories also drive meta-systems such as Monkey Knowledge trees (from memory; **unverified**).
- **Three upgrade paths with 5 tiers each.** A tower can use only 2 of the 3 paths, so crosspathing is a strategic choice. — https://www.bloonswiki.com/Centered_Path (search snippet)
- **Tier is visible on the model:** the Blade Shooter adds one blade to its emblem per path-1 upgrade (link above). Tier-5 forms get dramatic redesigns (for example, the Dart Monkey's path-3 tier 5 uses a colour scheme similar to the hero Quincy) — https://bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Dart_Monkey_(BTD6) (search snippet). Paragons fuse all three tier-5 looks.
- **What is enforced:** every tower is a monkey, every tower has 3×5 upgrades, and every category has a colour. **What is not:** the colour of the attack VFX.

### B2. Kingdom Rush (Ironhide): four archetypes, then specialisation

- **KR1–3:** four base families (Archer, Mage, Barracks, Artillery). Each upgrades 3 times, then branches into two specialist level-4 towers. For example, Mage, Adept and Wizard Tower branch into Arcane Wizard or Sorcerer Mage. — https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/KingdomRush ; https://wikily.gg/kingdom-rush/towers/mage-tower (search snippets). KR6 "Classic Rush" has 8 specialists built from level III base towers, two each. — https://wikily.gg/kingdom-rush-6-genesis/classic-towers (snippet)
- **Readability logic:** levels 1–3 are generic members of the family, so players learn just 4 silhouettes. The specialist at level 4 inherits the family base. Per a fan wiki, earlier towers were "non specialised for the first three stages", while Vengeance and Alliance towers "are specialised at every stage". — https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Upgrades (snippet)
- **Vengeance's shift:** it dropped the four-family branching for 18 tower types, bring 5 into a level, linear 3-level upgrades with an ability at the top. A review calls this "more basic towers with more linear upgrades". — https://androidauthority.com/kingdom-rush-vengeance-review-928335 ; https://www.gameskinny.com/fxzve/kingdom-rush-vengeance-tower-overview-guide (snippets)
- **Tier visibility:** a review of KR5 Alliance praises that "their visual style changes with each level up". — https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/kingdom-rush-5-alliance-td-review-lacking-depth-where-it-matters/ (snippet). Ironhide added an ultimate at the top level in KR5. — https://www.ironhidegames.com/News/Details/487 (snippet)
- No Ironhide designer quote on tower readability was found; the Kodeco interview doesn't cover it (https://www.kodeco.com/2769-making-a-hit-tower-defense-game-a-top-app-dev-interview).

### B3. League of Legends: written clarity rules (Riot, 2021)

From "Clarity in League" — https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/ :
- The silhouette is "the single most important" recognition factor. A good one has "a defining primary characteristic that's unique to that champion".
- The silhouette should show the **source of power** (a bow means ranged, a staff means caster). **Body size hints at role** (bulky reads as tank or fighter). **Facing direction** should be readable.
- "The amount of attention an ability grabs should match its level of importance." Importance is weighted by damage, hard CC, dodgeability and impact.
- "Projectiles should convey the direction they're traveling, even without motion."
- Skins: "A champion's primary feature should never be removed or significantly altered in any skin", and "Every ability should be at least as clear as the base skin".
- Abilities follow established shape language, for example knock-ups get strong vertical elements. — https://riotgames.com/en/news/dev-clarity-in-league (snippet)

From the 2017 League VFX Style Guide (via the VFX Apprentice summary, https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips ; original PDF https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/VFX_Styleguide_final_public_hidpjqwx7lqyx0pjj3ss.pdf):
- The priority order is gameplay clarity, then minimal clutter, then supporting the theme, then delight.
- "Every spell must feature a strict structural hierarchy split between primary and secondary elements". The primary elements carry gameplay at high value and opacity.
- Avoid pure black or pure white. Keep the peak brightness for the instant of impact.
- Saturation budget: environments are muted, UI is controlled, and VFX get "a wider, higher saturation ceiling".
- Colour associations: **healing leans green, frost blue, gunpowder orange-red**. "One color must drive the theme, while the complementary accent color is desaturated."
- The leading edge of a projectile is its brightest and sharpest part. The impact peaks within the first quarter of the effect's lifetime. Keep effects short.

### B4. Dota 2: value gradient

- Valve's workshop Character Art Guide says hero values run from dark at the feet to light at the head and upper body to pull the eye. Silhouette, value and colour together define the head, the weapon and the facing direction. Custom items may deviate only if the hero stays recognisable. — https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935 (the page body didn't render for me; this is from search-result summaries, so the details are secondary)

### B5. Hearthstone: class frame colours, and fixing collisions

- Every class card has a class-coloured frame, for example Druid's brown frame with thorn-like highlights. — https://hearthstone.fandom.com/wiki/Class (snippet)
- **Collision fix:** when Demon Hunter arrived with its own green, Hunter's frames were changed to a lighter green, "much closer to their World of Warcraft class color". — https://outof.games/hearthstone/1648-hearthstone-hunters-receive-lighter-green-card-frames-to-better-differentiate-from-demon-hunters
- **Consistency fix:** weapons used "the very faded border indicating which weapon belongs to each class". Blizzard: "With the introduction of Dual-Class cards, we went back and applied a class specific frame to every weapon in Hearthstone." — https://outof.games/hearthstone/1546-hearthstone-gets-new-weapon-frames-later-this-month-class-weapons-look-amazing
- **Lesson:** colour slots get crowded as a roster grows. Plan the palette for the final count, not the current one.

### B6. Magic: The Gathering: the colour pie as a mechanical contract

- Each ability has a **primary** colour (where it appears most, at the lowest rarity), sometimes a **secondary**, and sometimes a **tertiary** (rare, often restricted). For example, red is the colour that mainly deals direct damage, and "Black is the only color to do -N/-N abilities on spells." — https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/mechanical-color-pie-2021
- The pie is defined as much by what colours can't do. Green has no counterspells, blue no direct damage. — https://casualplaneswalker.com/wiki/color-pie (secondary)
- **Lesson:** a colour should mean "these verbs and not those". That lets players predict a new unit's role before reading it.

### B7. Slay the Spire: shape for type, colour for rarity, background for owner

- **Card type is the frame shape:** Attacks have "a rectangular border with the bottom edge being tapered down", Skills "a rectangular border", and Powers "an oval border". — https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Card
- **Rarity is the banner and frame colour:** grey for common, blue for uncommon, gold for rare. — same URL
- **Character is the background colour,** for example the Watcher's purple (inferred from asset names such as `BgAttackPurple.png`). Upgraded cards get a "+" suffix ("Eruption+"). — same URL
- **Lesson:** three independent visual channels (shape, banner colour, background colour) each carry one fact, and they never overlap.

### B8. Clash Royale: rarity frames, elixir cost

- Five rarities (Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Champion), each with a frame colour. Sources roughly agree on grey or blue for Common, orange for Rare, purple for Epic, a rainbow gradient for Legendary, and yellow or gold for Champion. — https://clashroyale.fandom.com/wiki/Cards (snippet) ; https://timesaver.gg/blog/clash-royale-cards-explained-rarities (snippet)
- In this game, rarity governs how hard a card is to collect and upgrade, not its power. All cards max at level 16. — search summary of https://timesaver.gg/blog/clash-royale-cards-explained-rarities
- Legendary cards having a distinct (hexagonal) frame shape is **(unverified)**; I couldn't confirm it.
- The elixir cost sits in a pink drop in the card corner (**unverified**, from memory).

### B9. Arknights: 8 classes, 72 branches

- There are 8 classes: Vanguard, Guard, Defender, Sniper, Caster, Medic, Supporter, Specialist. The class mostly decides **where** an operator deploys: Caster, Medic, Sniper and Supporter go on ranged tiles; Defender, Guard and Vanguard on melee tiles; Specialist on both. Some branches are exceptions. — https://arknights.wiki.gg/wiki/Class
- Each class has 6–14 **branches** (72 in total), and each branch has a one-line shared trait. For example, Chain Caster: "Attacks jump between multiple targets and temporarily Slow them, but deals 15% less damage in each jump." — same URL
- Each class and branch has its own icon (the wiki keeps separate icon categories). — https://arknights.wiki.gg/wiki/Template:Classes (snippet)
- **Tier display:** 1–6 stars for rarity. Elite 2 promotion unlocks new art. — https://arknights.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_Operator (snippet)
- **Lesson:** a two-level taxonomy (class, then branch) scales to hundreds of units. Players learn 8 class icons, and the branch trait text does the rest.

### B10. StarCraft: faction shape languages

- Samwise Didier (Blizzard art director): "Protoss… they're basically elliptical and circular. Terran are basically angular and square, and Zerg are very erratic and organic and spiky." His colour summary is "Gold, gray, brown." — https://www.engadget.com/2011/10/25/how-blizzard-makes-the-starcraft-2-races-so-iconic/
- A community member says he could show players "a never-before-seen HOTS unit and instantly someone could tell me if it was a Terran, Zerg or Protoss". — https://tl.net/forum/starcraft-2-hots/391805-spoilers-xelnaga-style-guide (snippet)
- **Lesson:** a family-level shape language (circles, squares, spikes) lets players classify a unit they have never seen.

### B11. Legion TD 2: damage types as a separate axis

- 4 attack types (pierce, impact, magic, pure) and 5 defence types (swift, fortified, natural, arcane, immaterial). The game shows the type icons on units, and an in-game matchup table is under F11. — https://legiontd2.wiki.gg/wiki/Armor_Types
- Legions (Element, Grove, Forsaken, Mech and others) are faction themes: Mech is high-tech AoE, Grove is regenerating nature spirits, Forsaken is undead synergies. — https://shapes.inc/fandom/legion-td-2 (secondary)
- **Lesson:** the faction (look) and the damage type (icon) are separate axes, so each can be read independently.

### B12. Patterns across all of the above

| Channel | What it encodes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Base silhouette or shape language** | family or role | KR 4 families; StarCraft circles, squares and spikes; LoL "source of power" |
| **Hue** | element, damage type, or owner/class | Pokémon types; Pikmin; MTG pie; Hearthstone class frame; BTD category icon |
| **Frame or border shape** | card or unit *type* | Slay the Spire attack, skill, power |
| **Frame colour** | rarity or tier | StS grey, blue, gold; Clash Royale |
| **Value and brightness** | importance, focal point | LoL VFX hierarchy; Dota dark-feet to light-head |
| **Ornament count** | upgrade level | BTD Blade Shooter blades; Arknights stars; Kirby none |
| **Size or redesign at the top tier** | ultimate tier | BTD tier 5 and Paragon; KR level 4 specialists; Arknights E2 art |
| **Icon badge** | class or branch | Arknights; Legion TD 2 damage types |

The common rule is **one channel per fact**. None of these games uses hue for both rarity and element.

## Rules we could adopt

These are concrete for Hollowlight, and each is tied to its precedent.

1. **Pick a template that is also a mechanic: every ward is a lantern-spirit.** One flame-spirit with a face lives in every ward, and the housing around it is the gear. Precedent: PvZ chose plants because they stay still (https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/piggybacking-2012-08-20), and BTD's monkeys with gear (https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Bloons_TD_6).

2. **One change per ward.** Keep the flame-spirit identical across wards, so only the housing or apparatus differs. Make that part big, because it is the role signal (crossbow arms, mortar bowl, frost globe, coil, long barrel, censer, prism, lamp-post). Precedent: Kirby swaps only the hat (https://gameranx.com/updates/id/372692/article/kirby-super-star-origins-revealed-by-sakurai-in-newest-video/).

3. **The housing silhouette must show the source of power and the range.** Long barrel means far, open bowl means area, wide arms means rapid. Squint-test every ward as a black silhouette at play size. Precedent: Riot's "source of power" and silhouette rules (https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/).

4. **Four families, four base shapes.** Group wards like BTD's four categories. One option: **Bolt** (single-target or projectile), **Blaze** (area, splash or burn), **Ward-craft** (control: slow, stun, chain) and **Hearth** (support, aura or economy). Give each family a base-plate shape, for example round, square, triangular and hexagonal. Precedent: BTD categories (https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Bloons_TD_6) and StarCraft's circles, squares and spikes (https://www.engadget.com/2011/10/25/how-blizzard-makes-the-starcraft-2-races-so-iconic/).

5. **Flame hue means damage family, and nothing else.** For example: warm gold for plain light or physical damage, orange-red for fire or burn, ice-blue for frost or slow, violet-white for lightning or chain, green for heal or support aura. Never reuse the hue for tier or rarity. Precedent: LoL's healing-green, frost-blue and gunpowder-orange associations (https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips), and Slay the Spire keeping rarity and type on separate channels (https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Card).

6. **Write a colour pie: each hue gets verbs it owns and verbs it never gets.** For example, frost-blue may slow but never deals the highest single-hit damage, and gold never gets area damage. Precedent: MTG's primary, secondary and tertiary abilities, and colour exclusives (https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/mechanical-color-pie-2021).

7. **Plan the palette for the final roster size.** Reserve hue slots for 20–30 wards now, so that ward #17 doesn't collide with ward #3. Precedent: Hearthstone had to relighten Hunter green when Demon Hunter arrived (https://outof.games/hearthstone/1648-hearthstone-hunters-receive-lighter-green-card-frames-to-better-differentiate-from-demon-hunters).

8. **Tier is shown by flame size, brightness and ornament count, never by hue.** For example, T1 is a small flame, T2 adds one ring or blade, T3 adds two, and T4 gets a full redesign with a halo. Precedent: BTD Blade Shooter adds one blade per upgrade (https://bloons.fandom.com/wiki/Blade_Shooter_(BTD6)), and Arknights uses star count (https://arknights.wiki.gg/wiki/Limited_Operator).

9. **The top tier is the only redesign, and it keeps the primary feature.** T4 can change dramatically, but the housing's defining trait (the barrel, the bowl) stays. Precedent: Riot's "A champion's primary feature should never be removed or significantly altered in any skin" (https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/), and Paragons fusing the parent's paths (https://www.bloonswiki.com/Paragons).

10. **Brightness is the attention budget.** Ward bodies sit at mid values. The brightest pixels on screen are reserved for the instant of impact and for the most dangerous effects. The Gloom stays dark and desaturated, so light effects always pop. Avoid pure white and pure black in sprites. Precedent: LoL VFX value range and saturation budget (https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips), and Dota's value gradient (https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/0688-7692-4D5A-1935). This fits the light-vs-darkness theme well.

11. **Projectiles show direction even in a still frame.** Make the leading edge brightest and give the trail a fading tail. Precedent: Riot's projectile rule (https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/) and the VFX guide's "leading edge" rule (https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips).

12. **Two-level taxonomy with a one-line trait.** Family icon, then the ward's one-line trait (at most 8 words), shown in the build menu. Precedent: Arknights branch traits (https://arknights.wiki.gg/wiki/Class), and George Fan's eight-word cap (https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-10-tutorial-tips-from-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-creator-george-fan).

13. **Give the template a face, and let it react.** The flame-spirit blinks, flinches when the Gloom gets close, and flares when it kills. Charm builds attachment for free. Precedent: Fan says blinking "really sold it", and Werner says "you feel for the guy" when the Wall-nut gets eaten (Escapist mirror: https://escapistmagazines.pages.dev/posts/the-sunny-side-of-the-zombiepocalypse).

14. **Admission test for each new ward: distinct verb, fiction first.** Before adding a ward, it must (a) own a verb that no other ward in its hue owns, and (b) come out of the light fiction (lamp, candle, lighthouse, sunbeam, firefly, mirror, ember), not just a stat gap. Cut ideas that are "too same-y or too complicated". Precedent: Supergiant on Demeter (https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-added-new-goddess-demeter-to-i-hades-i-pantheon), and PvZ 2 players' clone complaints (https://forums.ea.com/discussions/plants-vs-zombies-2-en/improving-plant-versatility-stop-the-clones-and-buff-classic-plants/13241276).

15. **Treat about 25 as the ceiling for one variation axis.** Past roughly 25 wards, add a second axis such as hybrids ("A's housing plus B's flame") instead of more one-off designs. Precedent: Kumazaki on the 25-copy-ability threshold (https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2016/09/kirby_planet_robobot_director_discusses_how_copy_abilities_are_chosen), Element TD dual and triple combinations (https://forums.eletd.com/topic/2681-characteristics-of-the-elemental-towers/), and Slime Rancher Largos (https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Monomi_Park).
